
=== atahsaia (lengkap-informasi, batch-148)
Átahsaia | tier core | giant | jenis raksasa | tradition-native-american
Ringkas: Átahsaia is a man-eating giant demon of Zuni religion and myth in the Southwestern United States. In Zuni tales he is slain by the twin war gods.
atahsaia-c01 (religious-tradition): Atahsaia is a giant cannibalistic demon in the religion and mythos of the Zuni people of the Southwestern United States.
atahsaia-c02 (traditional-belief): He is a giant, several times larger than a normal human being.
atahsaia-c03 (traditional-belief): In one story, he is depicted as so huge that he cannot fit his shoulders into a cave that a normal human easily passes.
atahsaia-c04 (traditional-belief): He is a cannibal, but besides eating his fellow demons he is also depicted as hungry for human flesh. He is also an inveterate liar.
atahsaia-c05 (scholarly-interpretation): According to anthropologist Elsie Clews Parsons, Átahsaia figures in a Zuni spiritual dance intended to teach children lessons about obedience.
atahsaia-c06 (scholarly-interpretation): In Zuni folklore, the a'doshlě is a "grandfather god" and the suukě a "grandmother god", representations of Átahsaia.
atahsaia-c07 (traditional-belief): In the story "Átahsaia, the Cannibal Demon", the demon lies to two young maidens who, believing he is their ugly grandfather, follow him to his home.
atahsaia-c08 (traditional-belief): In Cushing's telling, the cave is still called the "Cave of Átahsaia", and there Átahsaia himself lived in the times of the tale.
atahsaia-c09 (traditional-belief): In Cushing's telling his body was as big as the biggest elk's and his breast was shaggy with hair as stiff as porcupine quills.
atahsaia-c10 (traditional-belief): In Cushing's telling Átahsaia was ugly, could not speak without chattering his teeth or laugh without barking like a wolf, yet he was a very polite demon and a great liar.
atahsaia-c11 (traditional-belief): In the tale the character Mátsailéma says that there Átahsaia cuts the throats of his captives and casts their bones and heads into the depths of the chasm.
atahsaia-c12 (traditional-belief): In Cushing's telling, Áhaiyúta and Mátsailéma were the twin children of the Sun-father and the Mother Waters of the World.
atahsaia-c13 (traditional-belief): In the tale Áhaiyúta looses an arrow that, with a thundering noise and a rush of strong wind, buries itself in the demon's shoulders.
atahsaia-c14 (traditional-belief): In the tale Áhaiyúta tells the demon's head that it has been a liar and shall become a lying star.
atahsaia-c15 (traditional-belief): The twin Zuni war gods, Åhayúta and Mátsailéma, rescue the two young women and kill the demon.
