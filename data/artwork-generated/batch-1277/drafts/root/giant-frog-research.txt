
=== giant-frog (lengkap-informasi, batch-054)
giant frog | tier core | yokai | jenis yokai | japanese-folklore
Ringkas: The giant toad is a supernatural creature in Edo-period Japanese tale collections.
Kemampuan: null — It breathes out a rainbow-like vapor from its mouth; birds and insects touched by it are drawn into its mouth, and in summer it eats snakes. [giant-frog-c04]
giant-frog-c01 (traditional-belief): The giant toad is a supernatural creature in Edo-period Japanese tale collections.
giant-frog-c02 (traditional-belief): An Ehon Hyaku Monogatari tale places it in the mountains of Suō.
giant-frog-c03 (traditional-belief): That tale gives it a length of roughly 2.4 metres.
giant-frog-c04 (traditional-belief): It is said to draw birds and insects into its mouth through a rainbow-like breath.
giant-frog-c05 (scholarly-interpretation): Traditions of such giant toads are thought to have arisen because wild animals living in uncanny mountains can be much larger than those found in ordinary outdoor places.
giant-frog-c06 (scholarly-interpretation): There is an interpretation that toads were regarded as yōkai because the way a frog catches insects with its long tongue looks as if the insects are sucked into its mouth, so frogs and toads came to be thought to have uncanny powers such as sucking the spirit from people.
giant-frog-c07 (traditional-belief): In one tale, the thing taken for a rock on which a man named Fujita had been sitting was said to have opened fire-red eyes and yawned, then vanished without a trace; it was surmised to have been a giant toad and the bumps taken for rock to have been its warts.
giant-frog-c08 (historical-record): In the seventh month of Kyōwa 4 (August 1804), a giant toad appeared in "Tenjiku Tokubei Ikoku-banashi" by Tsuruya Nanboku, starring the first Onoe Matsusuke, at the Kawarazaki Theater in Edo, and was well received.
giant-frog-c09 (historical-record): Around Meiji 33, an Osaka newspaper reported daily on the "giant toad of Mount Shinoda"; that summer, a model of a human-sized toad with glaring big eyes in a thick gorge was shown at the shrine in Setomono-chō (陶器神社) and crowds gathered.
giant-frog-c10 (historical-record): Nichibunken image database description: this work by Ikkai Yoshitoshi shows Jiraiya, who uses toad sorcery, riding on a giant toad.
giant-frog-c11 (historical-record): Nichibunken image database description: in this work by Kōchōrō a giant toad is shown appearing behind Takiyasha-hime.
