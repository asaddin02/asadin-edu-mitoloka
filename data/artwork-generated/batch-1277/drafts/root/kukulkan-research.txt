
=== kukulkan (lengkap-informasi, batch-142)
Kukulkan | tier core | deity | jenis dewa | tradition-maya
Ringkas: Kukulkan is the feathered serpent deity of Maya mythology, closely related to the Aztec Quetzalcoatl. He was seen as a creator god who brought rain and winds, with a large temple at Chichen Itza.
Kemampuan: null — Kukulcan was regarded as a creator god who brought rain and winds; in modern folklore he travels ahead of Chaac and helps foretell the rains. [kukulkan-c18, kukulkan-c11]
kukulkan-c01 (scholarly-interpretation): It is closely related to the deity Qʼuqʼumatz of the Kʼicheʼ people and to Quetzalcoatl of Aztec mythology.
kukulkan-c02 (historical-record): Prominent temples to Kukulkan are found at archaeological sites in the Yucatán Peninsula, such as Chichen Itza, Uxmal and Mayapan.
kukulkan-c03 (scholarly-interpretation): The depiction of the Feathered Serpent is present in other cultures of Mesoamerica.
kukulkan-c04 (scholarly-interpretation): Although heavily Mexicanised, Kukulkan has its origins among the Maya of the Classic Period.
kukulkan-c05 (etymology): The Yucatec form of the name is formed from the word kuk 'feather' with the adjectival suffix -ul, giving kukul 'feathered', combined with kan 'snake', giving a literal meaning of 'feathered snake'.
kukulkan-c06 (scholarly-interpretation): Kukulkan has its origins among the Maya of the Classic Period, when it was known as Waxaklahun Ubah Kan, the War Serpent.
kukulkan-c07 (scholarly-interpretation): The cult of Kukulkan/Quetzalcoatl was the first Mesoamerican religion to transcend the old Classic Period linguistic and ethnic divisions and facilitated communication and peaceful trade among peoples of many different social and ethnic backgrounds.
kukulkan-c08 (historical-record): The cult was originally centred on the ancient city of Chichen Itza in the modern Mexican state of Yucatán and spread as far as the Guatemalan Highlands and northern Belize.
kukulkan-c09 (scholarly-interpretation): In Yucatán, references to the deity Kukulkan are confused with references to a historical individual who bore the god's name, who appears to have been a ruler or priest at Chichen Itza who first appeared around the 10th century.
kukulkan-c10 (historical-record): El Castillo at Chichen Itza served as a temple to Kukulkan. At the spring and fall equinoxes the shadow cast by the sun on the edges of the nine steps of the pyramid, combined with the northern stairway and the stone serpent head carvings, creates the illusion of a massive serpent descending the pyramid.
kukulkan-c11 (traditional-belief): A modern collection of folklore from Yucatán tells how Kukulkan was a winged serpent that flew to the sun and tried to speak to it, but the sun, in its pride, burnt his tongue; the same source says Kukulkan always travels ahead of the Yucatec Maya rain god Chaac, helping to predict the rains as his tail moves the winds and sweeps the earth clean.
kukulkan-c12 (traditional-belief): Among the Lacandon Maya of Chiapas, Kukulkan is an evil, monstrous snake that is the pet of the sun god.
kukulkan-c13 (traditional-belief): In one tale, Kukulkan is a boy who was born as a snake; as he grew older it became obvious that he was the plumed serpent, and his sister cared for him in a cave.
kukulkan-c14 (modern-popular-culture): In the 2006 film Apocalypto, the high priest in charge of human sacrifice prays to Kukulkan.
kukulkan-c15 (modern-popular-culture): In the 2002 video game Serious Sam: The Second Encounter, Kukulkan serves as a guardian of the time portal in Teotihuacan and is fought as that game's first boss.
kukulkan-c16 (religious-tradition): Kukulcan is the name of a feathered serpent god in the mythology and religion of Mesoamerica, in particular the Yucatec Maya; he is also identified as Quetzalcóatl by the Toltecs and Aztecs, as Gucumatz to the Quiché Maya of Guatemala, and as Ehecatl, the wind god of the Huastecs of the Gulf Coast.
kukulkan-c17 (religious-tradition): The chief god of the Yucatec Maya, who settled the Yucatan peninsula in the north of ancient Mesoamerica, was the feathered serpent Kukulcan, whose name means feathered (k'uk'ul) and serpent (kan); he was a creator god and the god of rain, wind, storms, and life.
kukulkan-c18 (religious-tradition): Kukulcan and his other manifestations are unified by the belief that each was considered a creator god and a bringer of rain and winds; the god is particularly associated with Chichen Itza, where a large temple was built in his honour.
kukulkan-c19 (historical-record): In addition to the god Kukulcan, the Yucatec Maya believed that a man called Kukulcan, a cultural hero who is either a ruler or priest, had some time in the 10th century CE (perhaps 987 CE) come to or even founded Chichen Itza in the northern tip of the Yucatan peninsula.
kukulkan-c20 (historical-record): On the pyramid, a staircase on each side rises the nine levels to a summit with a small square structure of two chambers; three stairways have 91 steps and the fourth has 92, making a total of 365, one step for each day of the year.
kukulkan-c21 (historical-record): The platform at the pyramid's top was used for religious ceremonies in honour of Kukulcan which included human sacrifices, a reminder that in Mesoamerican culture the gods had to be continuously fed and appeased to ensure the continuance and prosperity of the human race.
