
=== hatsadiling (lengkap-informasi, batch-075)
Hatsadiling | tier core | bird | jenis hewan mitos | tradition-thai
Ringkas: Hatsadiling is a mythical bird commonly found in Northern Thai art, described as house-sized with a lion's head and body, an elephant's trunk and a bird's wings, and the strength of five elephants.
Kemampuan: supernatural-strength — Buddhaghosa mentions it as an animal possessing the strength of five elephants; a paper likewise describes the bird as being as powerful as five elephants. [hatsadiling-c06, hatsadiling-c07]
hatsadiling-c01 (traditional-belief): Hatsadiling is a mythical bird commonly found in Northern Thai art.
hatsadiling-c02 (traditional-belief): Its form combines a lion, an elephant's trunk and tusks, a cock's comb and bird wings.
hatsadiling-c03 (traditional-belief): Northeastern Thai oral tradition places this bird in Himavanta forest.
hatsadiling-c04 (traditional-belief): Its motif appears on funerary hearses for prominent monks in cremation ceremonies.
hatsadiling-c05 (historical-record): The Camadevivamsa features the bird in the account of Hariphunchai's founding.
hatsadiling-c06 (traditional-belief): Buddhaghosa describes it as possessing the strength of five elephants.
hatsadiling-c07 (scholarly-interpretation): According to the paper's abstract, a characteristic of the Hatsadiling bird is its big shape and being as powerful as five elephants; it is said to have originated in the good land of the so-called moral people, the Uttarakuru Continent.
hatsadiling-c08 (scholarly-interpretation): According to the paper's abstract, the Buddhist texts and literature only mention the bird's big shape and five elephants' worth of power, and that it took people away because it thought they were victims, with no intention of harm.
hatsadiling-c09 (scholarly-interpretation): According to the paper's abstract, Hatsadiling appears in the Tipitaka and many Atthakathas (Commentaries), including Buddhist literature such as Tom-nan Mun Sasana (The Legend of Original Religion).
hatsadiling-c10 (scholarly-interpretation): According to the paper's abstract, these concepts have influenced paintings and sculptures of the Hatsadiling Bird Castle at crematoriums.
hatsadiling-c11 (scholarly-interpretation): According to the paper's abstract, an imagination of special shapes was added to the concept, including Pritsanā Tham to remember the goodness of the dead and to transmit them to be born in heaven.
hatsadiling-c12 (traditional-belief): In the Thai language the bird is also known by a number of terms, including nok hatsading (นกหัสดิน), nok hatsadi (นกหัสดี) and nok hat.
hatsadiling-c13 (traditional-belief): The hatsadiling (hathi linga) has also been used by the Marma people as a primary motif for funerary hearses.
