
=== vision-serpent (lengkap-informasi, batch-144)
Vision Serpent | tier core | dragon | jenis naga/ular mitos | tradition-maya
Ringkas: The Vision Serpent is a giant serpent of pre-Columbian Maya mythology that appeared in visions during bloodletting rituals, serving as a gateway to the spirit realm through which ancestors and gods manifested themselves.
Kemampuan: null — In ritual visions the giant serpent served as a gateway to the spirit realm through which ancestors or gods manifested themselves. [vision-serpent-c05, vision-serpent-c06]
vision-serpent-c01 (religious-tradition): The Vision Serpent is an important creature of pre-Columbian Maya mythology, though the term is becoming outdated.
vision-serpent-c02 (religious-tradition): Maya mythology makes serpents the vehicles of the sun and stars across the sky; their shedding made them symbols of rebirth.
vision-serpent-c03 (scholarly-interpretation): The Vision Serpent is thought the most important Maya serpent.
vision-serpent-c04 (scholarly-interpretation): It was usually bearded with a rounded snout, and was frequently shown double-headed, or with a god's or ancestor's spirit coming out of its mouth.
vision-serpent-c05 (religious-tradition): In Maya bloodletting rituals, participants experienced visions in which they communicated with ancestors or gods; the visions took the form of a giant serpent serving as a gateway to the spirit realm.
vision-serpent-c06 (religious-tradition): The Vision Serpent became the way ancestors or gods manifested themselves to the Maya, a direct link between the spirit realm of the gods and the physical world.
vision-serpent-c07 (religious-tradition): Once the bloodletting was over, the blood-soaked ceremonial papers were burned, releasing a column of smoke that provided the perfect medium for the Vision Serpent to appear.
vision-serpent-c08 (scholarly-interpretation): The Vision Serpent goes back to earlier Maya conceptions and lies at the center of the world as they conceived it.
vision-serpent-c09 (traditional-belief): There was a Vision Serpent named Och-Kan, lord of Kalak'mul.
vision-serpent-c10 (historical-record): Around 1930 J.E.S. Thompson noted in San Antonio, Belize, that the Q'eqchi' Maya still performed a ritual very similar to the classic Maya vision quest, in a different context; it marked the initiation of a new shaman for the village.
vision-serpent-c11 (historical-record): The Hauberg Stela (A.D. 199) from the Maya Lowlands is described as one of the first dated monuments depicting the Vision Serpent's connection to bloodletting.
vision-serpent-c12 (historical-record): A scene from Temple 23 shows a giant double-headed snake creature from whose mouths emerge a warrior and the war and rain god Tlaloc over a kneeling worshipper, Lady Xok', wife of the Yaxchilan ruler Shield-Jaguar, who sees the monster in a blood-letting induced vision.
vision-serpent-c13 (religious-tradition): Bloodletting was also, though less often, performed in order to communicate with ancestors.
